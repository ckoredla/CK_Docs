# Editorial and Whitepaper Standard

Every public article must be useful enough to stand on its own and rigorous enough to survive technical review. Volume never overrides credibility.

## Publication classes

### Research note
A concise original analysis used in the historical technology archive. It may establish a thesis and architecture lens, but it must not be promoted as a whitepaper until the whitepaper gate is complete.

### Flagship article
A fully researched Medium/Substack-quality article with a clear argument, primary-source citations, original diagrams, implementation guidance, limitations, and editorial review.

### Whitepaper
A durable, evidence-led publication that includes an executive summary, problem definition, assumptions, reference architecture, trade-offs, controls, implementation model, measurable outcomes, limitations, and complete references.

## Originality requirements

1. Begin from an original question, thesis, and outline.
2. Use multiple primary sources rather than paraphrasing one article.
3. Never copy sentences, diagrams, tables, examples, or distinctive structure without permission and attribution.
4. Place direct quotations in quotation marks, keep them brief, and cite the source.
5. Distinguish sourced fact, author analysis, recommendation, and hypothetical example.
6. Run similarity review before publication. A passing automated score never replaces manual review.
7. Retain research notes and source links in the repository for auditability.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Standards, regulations, official government material, and peer-reviewed research.
2. Official product documentation, architecture guides, and engineering publications.
3. Public technical reports and conference papers from recognized organizations.
4. High-quality independent analysis used for context, never as the sole support for important claims.

Every material factual claim must be traceable. Unsupported precision is worse than an honest qualification.

## Required article structure

1. Title and accurate summary
2. Executive perspective
3. Problem and decision to improve
4. Context and evidence
5. Original architecture diagram
6. Design choices and trade-offs
7. Security, reliability, governance, and operating model
8. Implementation approach
9. Metrics and expected outcomes
10. Failure modes and limitations
11. Practical recommendations
12. References and methodology note

## Visual standard

Each flagship article or whitepaper must contain at least two purposeful visuals:

- One original system, data-flow, sequence, operating-model, or decision architecture
- One supporting visual such as a lifecycle, decision matrix, comparison table, maturity model, or measurement framework

Visuals must:

- Explain boundaries, decisions, data movement, ownership, or trade-offs
- Use consistent typography, spacing, labels, and direction
- Remain readable on desktop and mobile
- Include a caption and accessibility description
- Be created specifically for this publication
- Avoid decorative vendor-logo clouds and copied marketing graphics

## Whitepaper quality gate

A page may be labeled `Whitepaper` only when all items pass:

- [ ] Original thesis and author perspective
- [ ] Primary-source research packet retained
- [ ] Every material claim cited
- [ ] No copied prose or unlicensed visual material
- [ ] Topic-specific architecture diagram
- [ ] At least one additional analytical visual
- [ ] Trade-offs, limitations, and failure modes included
- [ ] Security, reliability, and governance addressed
- [ ] Editorial and technical review completed
- [ ] Mobile and desktop visual review completed
- [ ] Author signoff recorded

## Distribution rule

The website is the canonical source. LinkedIn, Medium, DEV, Substack, and newsletter versions are adaptations created only after the canonical article passes review. Historical archive dates must remain clearly identified as retrospective dates rather than false publication history.
